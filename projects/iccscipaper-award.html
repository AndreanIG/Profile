Image Pre-Processing Effect on OCR's Performance for Image Conversion to Braille Unicode

An award-winning study on how image pre-processing pipelines affect OCR accuracy when converting printed text into Braille Unicode for visually impaired readers.

Paper

Ignasius, A., Chandra, J. C., Oscadinata, R., & Suhartono, D. (2023). Image Pre-Processing Effect on OCR's Performance for Image Conversion to Braille Unicode. ICCSCI, Procedia Computer Science, vol. 227, pp. 922–931. Elsevier. DOI: 10.1016/j.procs.2023.10.599.

Problem

Braille remains the primary reading medium for many visually impaired people, but converting printed material into Braille is slow and labour-intensive. Automating the conversion with OCR is promising, yet OCR is notoriously sensitive to image quality. This research measured exactly which pre-processing techniques make text-to-Braille conversion more accurate.

Approach

  • Pipeline: compared combinations of grayscale conversion, noise reduction, thresholding/binarization, and skew correction before running OCR.
  • Engine: used Tesseract OCR to recognise scanned text and map recognised characters to Braille Unicode.
  • Evaluation: measured recognition accuracy across multiple pre-processing configurations on a dataset of scanned text images.

Results

  • Identified the combination of filtering and thresholding steps that maximises OCR precision.
  • Demonstrated that specific pre-processing choices significantly improve the reliability of Braille Unicode generation.
  • Presented the findings at ICCSCI and received the Best Research Paper Award.

Why It Matters

Small computational choices can make a real difference in accessibility technology. Improving OCR for Braille conversion helps more people read, learn, and communicate independently, and this research shaped how I approach detail-oriented, user-centred ML work.
